# Tidal Script

Writing that nobody wrote: the slow current underneath a page, made visible by the ink it carries.
Each strand begins as a seeded point and is combed forward by a field of noise, so the drawing is
the record of a force rather than a picture of anything. The strands crowd into a loose band like a
tide line and thin out towards the margins, where the paper is left alone.

Motion is a drift, not a performance: the field turns once around a small circle in noise space
over eight seconds and comes back to where it began, so the loop has no seam. Nothing is added or
taken away between frames; every frame is the same strands, re-traced through a field that has
moved a little.

Colour is four inks on warm paper (deep blue, sea green, rust and near-black), with the rare inks
chosen least often so that the eye finds them. Strand weights vary within a narrow range, enough to
read as a hand without becoming noise.

The parameters are the qualities of the current: how many strands, how far each is traced, the
scale and swirl of the field, and the radius of the tide. The seed chooses where the ink lands.
